Setup Checklist

Complete before your first day
Before You Start

Get These Right First

If you skip setup, you’ll spend your first month fixing problems instead of closing deals. Complete every item below before moving to Module 1.

Warning: If you delete a board, rename a status, or modify sequences before understanding the system, your automations will break. This is the #1 support complaint. Don’t customize until you can run the default build consistently.

1. DataSift Account — Default Build

  • Confirm your account has the default build loaded: 5 Kanban boards (Lead Management, Acquisitions, Transactions, Wholesale Flips, Rentals)
  • Confirm property statuses are intact: New Lead, No Contact New Lead, Cold, Warm, Hot, Ghosting Lead, Dead Lead, Not Interested, Under Contract, Closed
  • Confirm sequences are intact (26–27 default sequences)
  • If anything is missing, contact DataSift support — they can press a button and reload the default build

2. Phone System / Dialer

  • Set up your phone provider (smrtPhone, Twilio, or another dialer)
  • Create 3 separate inboxes in your phone system:
    • Inbound Leads — for direct mail responses
    • Online Inbounds — for website / SEO / PPC leads
    • PPL Inbounds — for pay-per-lead provider responses (if applicable)
  • Assign tracking numbers to the correct inboxes
  • Confirm your lead manager knows which inbox to check for each lead source

3. Task Presets — Assign to Your Lead Manager

  • Go to Event System → Presets in DataSift
  • Open the Lead Management folder
  • Edit each preset: change the assignee from Sensei (account owner) to User Round Robin
  • Select your lead manager(s) in the round robin list
Why User Round Robin? When you hire a second lead manager later, you just add them to the round robin — no need to re-edit every preset.

4. Custom Fields (75 Fields, 7 Folders)

  • Install the DataSift Lead Management plugin in Claude Cowork (download from the Help Center)
  • Run the Setup skill first: say “set up my training”
  • Run the Custom Fields skill: say “build my custom fields” — auto-creates all 7 folders and 75 fields in about 60 seconds
  • Verify in Settings → Custom Fields → Fields tab that all 7 folders appear: Qualifying Questions, Property Condition, CapEx Assessment, Property Debts & Encumbrances, Title & Ownership, Deal Intelligence, Appointment & Next Steps

5. KPI Tracking — Google Sheet + Tally Counter

  • Create or copy the KPI Google Sheet with a Lead Manager tab
  • Columns: Date, Total Dials, Conversations, Sent to Acquisitions, Same-Day Contact %, Leads Missing Tasks
  • Download a Tally Counter app on your phone — tally in real time throughout the day
  • Transfer numbers to the Google Sheet before logging off — do not batch weekly

6. Daily Workflow Filters

Set up these saved filters in your DataSift Records page. These are the filters your lead manager uses every day, in order:

  1. Call New Lead Tasks — Task preset: Call New Lead, Due: Overdue + Due Today
  2. Unqualified Lead Tasks — Status: No Contact New Lead, Due: Overdue + Due Today
  3. Qualified Lead Tasks — Status: Cold + Warm + Hot, Due: Overdue + Due Today
  4. Leads Missing Tasks — Status: Cold + Warm + Hot + No Contact New Lead, Tasks: 0
  5. Send Back to Lead Management — Task preset: Send Back to LM, Due: Overdue + Due Today
These filters ARE your daily workflow. If your lead manager runs through these every day in order, they’re doing their job.

Welcome & The Role

~20 minutes
The Big Picture

What We Do

Florida Cash Real Estate is a full-service real estate business. We buy houses from motivated sellers, renovate them, and either sell them to end buyers or add them to our rental portfolio. The lead manager is the engine that makes the entire operation work.

Most people hear "we buy houses" and think we lowball distressed homeowners. That is not what we do. We solve real problems for real people — homeowners dealing with foreclosure, probate, divorce, code violations, or properties they simply cannot afford to maintain. We are problem solvers first, investors second.

Where Leads Come From

Leads enter the business from four primary sources. Each has a different profile and urgency level.

1

Direct Mail

Postcards and letters to targeted lists (high equity, pre-foreclosure, probate).

Sellers respond by calling or texting. These are warm inbounds — they initiated the contact. Process through smrtPhone inbound inbox.

2

Cold Calling

Outbound team (callers) dials through targeted lists and generates leads.

These are outbound-generated leads. The cold caller had a conversation and determined the seller might be interested. They push the record to you as a New Lead. Quality varies — you need to verify motivation.

3

Carrot (Website)

Sellers find our website through Google and submit their property info.

These are high-intent leads — the seller searched for a solution and found us. Process through carrot.com and the smrtPhone "Online Inbounds" inbox. Speed to contact matters here.

4

Pay-Per-Lead (PPL)

Third-party lead providers send us seller inquiries for a fee per lead.

Process through propertyleads.com and the smrtPhone "PPL Inbounds" inbox. These sellers are actively looking to sell — they went to a lead provider site and filled out a form. Treat these as hot inbounds.

Your Role as Lead Manager

You are the first human touchpoint in the sales cycle. Your job is to take every lead that enters the business and move it through a structured qualification process until it either becomes an appointment for the Acquisitions Specialist or exits the pipeline.

The core goal of every interaction: End with either an appointment set for the Acquisitions Specialist or a confirmed follow-up task with a date. No lead should ever sit without a next action. There is no third option.

You are not making offers or closing deals. You are gathering intelligence on the seller's situation, maintaining consistent follow-up, and making sure no lead ever sits without a next action. You are the bridge between marketing (which generates the leads) and Acquisitions (which makes offers and closes deals).

Your Toolbox — DataSift

~30 minutes
The Default Build

What Comes Pre-Built

Every DataSift account ships with a complete framework for managing your sales cycle. This default build was developed through direct collaboration with six- and seven-figure investors. Your job is to learn and operate within it — then improve it over time.

1

SiftLine Boards

Kanban-style boards for visualizing your pipeline.

Default boards: Lead Management, Acquisitions, Transactions, Wholesale, Flips, and Rentals. SiftLine is for managing records after they become leads — not for prospecting large volumes of unqualified data.

2

Property Statuses

Where a record sits in the sales cycle.

Active statuses: New Lead, No Contact New Lead, Cold, Warm, Hot, Ghosting Lead, Dead Lead, Not Interested, Under Contract, Closed. Others exist but are toggled off by default.

3

Task Presets

Pre-configured follow-up actions with assignees and due dates.

  • Lead Management: Call New Lead, No Contact New Lead, Nurture New Lead, Cold/Warm/Hot Follow-Up
  • Acquisitions: Make Offer, Offer Follow-Up, Send Back to Lead Management
  • Transactions: Process New Contract, Follow Up with Seller and Title, Close Out Deal
4

Sequences

Automations that fire when statuses change or cards move.

Sequences follow a Trigger → Condition → Action pattern. When you move a card to the Cold Lead phase, a sequence fires that changes the status, clears existing tasks, and adds a Cold Follow-Up due in 45 days. When you complete that task, the loop creates another one automatically.

Important: A sequence cannot be triggered by an action from another sequence. Combine actions into one sequence instead.

Be careful about expanding the system. Do not create new statuses, tasks, or phases just because you feel like you need them. The default build was designed to focus you on what matters. Expanding without understanding why it's built this way will create clutter, break automations, and make your pipeline harder to manage.

The Non-Negotiable Habit

STABM — Check Every Record, Every Time

Every time you open or leave a record, run STABM. This is not a QC audit step — it is a proactive habit on every record you touch. If you STABM every record on the way in and on the way out, you are doing quality control in real-time throughout your day.

S
Status
Is the property status correct for where this record is in the pipeline?
T
Task
Is the right task on the record? Only one task? Correct frequency?
A
Assignee
Is the record assigned to the correct team member?
B

Correct SiftLine board and phase? Does it match the status and task?

STABM prevents problems from accumulating. Status-phase mismatches, orphaned records, and double tasks happen when people skip this step. Make it muscle memory.

The Lead Flow

~35 minutes
Three Phases

How Every Lead Moves Through the Pipeline

Every lead moves through three phases: New Lead, Unqualified, and Qualified. Within each phase, the record has a specific status, task, and follow-up frequency. Leads are always in motion — they promote or demote based on engagement and never sit static.

Phase 1: New Lead

A brand-new lead has entered the business. Call immediately. This phase should last one day. By end of day, the New Lead column on SiftLine should be empty.

ComponentValue
StatusNew Lead
TaskCall New Lead
FrequencyDue immediately (by 6:00 PM the day it comes in)
Duration1 day — clear same day

Outcomes: They answer → qualify and move to Cold/Warm/Hot. They don't answer → move to No Contact New Lead (Phase 2). Property is clearly not viable → Dead Lead. They say no after objection handling → Not Interested.

Phase 2: Unqualified

The lead has expressed interest (or was identified as a potential seller), but you haven't spoken to them yet. Two stages with different tempos.

Stage 1: No Contact New Lead (Daily)
ComponentValue
StatusNo Contact New Lead
TaskNo Contact New Lead
FrequencyDue daily
Duration3–5 days (your judgment)

Call every day. Double-tap (morning and evening). Text. Leave voicemails. Duration depends on the property — a distressed block home from an inbound Carrot lead gets the full 5 days. A rural lot with $3K comps might get 3 days.

Stage 2: Nurture New Lead (Weekly)
ComponentValue
TaskNurture New Lead
FrequencyDue weekly
DurationUp to 3–6 months

Reduced frequency. The task loops automatically — complete it, a new one gets added. A high-value property stays in nurture for 6 months. Marginal ones, 1–3 months before moving to Ghosting.

Total touches in unqualified: 5 days daily + 6 months weekly = ~31 touches before Ghosting. You are not giving up — you're managing your time by adjusting frequency.

Phase 3: Qualified

You've spoken to the seller and qualified them. Now assign a temperature based on motivation, timeline, and engagement.

Hot — Every 2 Days

Actively communicating, wants to sell, good lead for your business. Hot is about tempo and priority, not just motivation level.

Warm — Every 15 Days

Moderate motivation. Timeline 30–180 days. Some flexibility on price. Engaging but not ready to commit.

Cold — Every 45 Days

Wants to sell but not urgent. Timeline 180+ days. Price may be anchored high. Motivation is low but present.

Special case — Foreclosure or auction with a deadline: Even if the seller hasn't expressed strong motivation, if the property is going to auction in 30 days, common sense says you keep it on a hot or daily tempo. The situation dictates the frequency, not just the seller's words.

Pipeline distribution: A healthy qualified pipeline is roughly ~75% Cold, ~15% Warm, ~5% Hot. This makes sense because cold has the longest interval and most leads naturally settle there over time.

Temperature Movement

Leads Are Always in Motion

Leads promote or demote based on engagement. They never just sit static at one temperature.

Promotion (Temperature Up)

  • Cold → Warm: Seller becomes more engaged, timeline shortens
  • Warm → Hot: Seller is ready to discuss an offer, actively communicating
  • Any qualified → Send to Acquisitions: Seller is ready for an offer

Demotion (Temperature Down)

  • Hot → Warm: Seller stops communicating after ~1 week at hot tempo
  • Warm → Cold: Still not engaging after a period on 15-day tempo
  • Cold → Ghosting: After 12–18 months of cycling with no meaningful engagement

Terminal Statuses

These are the exit points from the active pipeline. Each has a specific meaning — do not use them interchangeably.

Dead Lead

Click to flip

Clearly never going to be a deal. Unrealistic expectations, property not viable, or outbound team pushed it but after reviewing, it's automatically not viable. No task added — handled through marketing filters.

Not Interested

Click to flip

You spoke to the seller. They explicitly said they do not want to sell. You went through your objection list and they confirmed. This is NOT "we couldn't reach them." Requires a conversation. No task added — gets periodic marketing campaigns.

Lost Deal

Click to flip

At any point, the property got listed with an agent or sold to another investor. The deal is gone. No task added — move to Lost Deal phase on SiftLine.

Ghosting

Click to flip

The lead stopped communicating over time. No active task, but they receive periodic marketing campaigns every 45–90 days. Ghosting is not permanent — if they respond, they re-enter at the appropriate qualified status.

The Handoff to Acquisitions

When a qualified lead is ready for an offer, move the record to the Send to Acquisitions phase on the Lead Management SiftLine board. This triggers a sequence that creates a Make Offer task and moves the card to the Acquisitions board. If the lead comes back, it enters the Sent Back to LM phase and re-enters your workflow — usually at Warm or Hot, since they were recently engaged enough to warrant an offer.

Your Day — Daily Workflow

~25 minutes
Priority Order

How Your Day Flows

Work through these in order every day. Use the filter presets on the Records page under the Lead Management folder. For each filter, always check Overdue first, then switch to Due Today.

One rule overrides everything: Any new inbound call or newly submitted lead takes immediate priority over whatever you are currently working on. Inbounds always come first.

  1. Clear the Queue

    Missed calls, voicemails, texts. Check inbound direct mail leads (smrtPhone "Inbound Leads"), PPL leads (propertyleads.com + smrtPhone "PPL Inbounds"), Carrot leads (carrot.com + smrtPhone "Online Inbounds").

  2. Call New Lead Tasks (Preset 0)

    Brand-new leads needing first call attempt. Complete same day. New Lead column should be empty by end of day.

  3. Unqualified Lead Tasks (Preset 1)

    No Contact New Lead (daily follow-up) and Nurture New Lead (weekly). Call, double-tap, text, voicemails.

  4. Qualified Follow-Ups (Preset 2)

    Hot (2 days), Warm (15 days), Cold (45 days). Work overdue first, then due today.

  5. Leads Missing Tasks (Preset 3)

    Daily safety net. Any lead without a task is invisible to your workflow. Should be zero every day.

  6. Sent Back to LM (Preset 5)

    Leads returned from Acquisitions. Review, re-qualify, put back at the right temperature with the correct task.

End of Day Checklist

Before You Log Off

  • Check for missed calls, voicemails, or texts that need a response
  • Confirm New Lead phase on SiftLine is empty
  • Confirm all tasks due today are completed or rescheduled
  • Transfer Tally Count numbers to the KPI Google Sheet

Common Mistakes

  • Leaving New Leads overnight — these must be contacted same day
  • Skipping the Leads Missing Tasks filter — invisible leads are lost leads
  • Batching KPI entry at end of week — daily entry keeps data accurate
  • Forgetting to log call notes — do it immediately after each call

Custom Fields & The Intake Call

~20 minutes
The Source of Truth

Seven Custom Field Folders

When you get a seller on the phone, it's not enough to assign a temperature and set a follow-up. You need to capture detailed information into seven custom field folders in DataSift. These fields are the singular source of truth for the entire company.

1

Qualifying Questions

Timeline, motivation, asking price, flexibility.

This is where you capture the seller's urgency, their price expectations, and how flexible they are. These fields directly determine the lead temperature.

2

Property Condition

Overall condition, vacancy, interior/exterior notes.

General assessment of the property. Is it occupied or vacant? What's the overall condition? Any visible issues?

3

CapEx Assessment

Roof, HVAC, water heater, plumbing, electrical, junk removal.

Every "Unknown" in CapEx forces conservative assumptions that lower the offer. The better the intake, the more accurate the underwriting, the more competitive the offer.

4

Property Debts

Mortgage, taxes, liens, HOA, probate, foreclosure.

What does the seller owe? Are there liens? Is there an HOA with violations? Is the property in probate or foreclosure? This affects deal structure.

5

Title & Ownership

Decision-maker confirmation, heirs, trusts, attorneys.

Who actually has authority to sell? Is there a trust? Multiple heirs? An attorney involved? If you're not talking to the decision-maker, you're wasting time.

6

Deal Intelligence

Price source, willingness, preferred terms, negotiation intel.

Where did the seller's price come from? Zillow estimate? Agent appraisal? Their own head? Are they open to creative terms? This is gold for the Acquisitions Specialist.

7

Appointment & Next Steps

Photos on file, field visit scheduling, property access logistics.

Do we have photos? Has a field visit been scheduled for the TC or team member? Who will be present? Is there lockbox access? Appointment dates and times live in the Tasks feature — this folder captures the access logistics and field visit status.

Update custom fields during or immediately after every seller conversation. Don't leave blanks for Acquisitions to discover later. The fields are your intake guide — refer to CUSTOM_FIELDS.md for the complete field-by-field breakdown and the CapEx cheat sheet.

Quality Control

~15 minutes
Pipeline Hygiene

Keep the Pipeline Clean

QC is a daily discipline, not an occasional audit. STABM handles real-time QC. The four audit filters below are your weekly safety net — they catch anything that slipped through.

Boards, Tasks, Check

Finds records on a board phase that have overdue or due-today tasks. Look for overdue tasks that haven't been addressed, conflicting tasks (a Make Offer task on a No Contact record), or records moved between phases without sequences updating. Fix by running STABM and resolving what's out of place.

Boards, No Tasks, Check

Finds records on a board phase that have zero tasks. A record on the board with no task is invisible. It will never surface in your daily workflow. This filter is the only way to find them. Usually caused by a manual status change that didn't trigger the sequence, or a task loop that didn't add the next one.

Boards with Two or More Tasks

Finds records with multiple tasks. Usually a double task (one manual, one from a sequence), tasks from a previous phase that weren't cleared, or random tasks stacked on top of the follow-up task. Keep the most recent and correct task. Delete the duplicate.

Boards Missing Correct Number

Finds records that don't have a correct phone number. These records will fail in drip campaigns and dial sessions. Flag them for skip tracing or further research.

The #1 QC Problem: Status vs. Phase Mismatch

The single most frequent QC issue is records where the status doesn't match the board phase. Example: a record in the Cold Lead phase but with a "Not Interested" status. This happens when someone changes the status manually instead of moving the card on SiftLine.

The fix: Always move cards on the board instead of just changing statuses manually. Let the sequences do their job. For bulk cleanup: filter by a phase, then add a second filter for status "does not include" that phase's status. Everything that shows up is mismatched.

Drip Campaigns

~15 minutes
Automated Follow-Up

Keeping the Line Alive

Drip campaigns automate follow-up on records that no longer need manual effort. The primary use case: leads that have exited the active pipeline — ghosting and not interested leads — where you still want periodic contact without burning calling hours.

Drips supplement your manual workflow — they do not replace it. Your daily workflow (filter presets, manual task loops) handles active leads. Drips pick up where manual effort leaves off.

Ghosting Lead Nurture

45-day cycle, 4 cycles total (~142 days). Triggered when a record moves to the Ghosting phase on SiftLine.

45-day delay
Cycle 2 SMS
Hey @Owner First Name, Jay here. Your property at @Property Address is still on my radar. Text or call me if you want to talk.
45-day delay
Cycle 3 SMS
Hi @Owner First Name, just checking in on @Property Address. If the timing is ever right to sell, my number hasnt changed. -Jay
45-day delay
Cycle 4 SMS
Hey @Owner First Name, its Jay. Periodically checking in on @Property Address. If anything has changed, Id love to connect.

Not Interested Nurture

90-day cycle with a 14-day initial buffer (respects the seller's stated decision), 4 cycles total (~360 days). Triggered when a record moves to the Not Interested phase.

14-day buffer before first text

SMS templates follow the same tone — casual, no pressure, acknowledging they said no but leaving the door open. 90-day spacing between each touch. Each cycle includes an SMS plus a manual task for the lead manager to call.

Critical Design Rules

!
Drips Run on Autopilot

Once enrolled, actions fire on schedule whether the lead manager notices or not. You need a plan for how leads get removed from drips when they re-engage.

!
Sequence-Based Removal Is Essential

When a ghosting lead re-enters the pipeline, the receiving sequence (New Lead, Cold, Warm, Hot) must include a "Remove from Drip Campaign" action. Without this, double-contact.

!
No Task Conflicts

Drip campaigns are for leads that exited the active task-based pipeline. Don't add drip-generated tasks to records that already have sequence-generated task loops.

Questions & Answers

These are real questions from DataSift customers, answered live during training. If you have the same question, you’re not alone.

Never say “my partner will be in touch” — it’s vague and doesn’t build trust. Instead, use real names and real roles:

  • If you have 2–3 people, name them: “I’m going to pass your information to either Jay, Danny, or Brent — one of them will be reaching out.”
  • If you have more, summarize: “Someone from our lead management team — whoever’s quickest available — will reach out. Keep an eye out for a call from an 850 number you don’t recognize. We’ll text you before we call.”

Setting this expectation is what separates a 50%+ qualifying rate from sitting in the 20s. The seller needs to know what happens next, who’s contacting them, and from what number.

There are two kinds of motivation: what the seller tells you, and what the data tells you.

  • No motivation + no data indicators (owner-occupied, high equity, unrealistic price, no distress): Not a real lead. Move to Not Interested or Dead Lead. This is a qualifying problem on the caller side.
  • “No motivation” + data indicators (probate, absentee, auction date coming): This IS a lead. The seller may say they want $650K, but if they’re in probate and absentee, there may be a listing opportunity or a future cash offer.

If someone says they’re interested in selling, they’re a lead — because you may have multiple ways to close (cash offer, listing, creative terms). If they’re completely off in left field with zero indicators, move them to Dead Lead with “unrealistic expectations” as the reason.

If it’s occasional: Put them in the follow-up frequency that matches how closely you want to track them. This is typically a warm lead — check in regularly: “Hey, just checking to see if you found a place. Can I send you a few recommendations?”

Key insight: Cold, warm, and hot aren’t about when the seller wants to sell. They’re about the frequency you need to follow up. A probate property 6 months out might be hot because it’s in a neighborhood you want to buy in.

Final Takeaways

Keys to Success

These are the principles that separate good lead managers from great ones.

  1. Every call ends with an appointment or a follow-up task.

    There is no third option. An appointment means the lead goes to Acquisitions. A follow-up task means you have a date to call back.

  2. Comping is the enemy of revenue.

    There's selling time and admin time. Master having a general idea of property value so you can understand seller needs without getting bogged down in analysis during calling hours.

  3. Stay organized.

    Know who you need to speak with and when. Tasks must be updated constantly. Every lead should have a task unless it's Dead or has an Appointment set for Acquisitions.

  4. Call back every lead the same business day it comes in.

    After-hours hot leads? Motivated lead managers call those back too.

  5. Always take calls with DataSift open.

    Log notes immediately. Update custom fields during the call. Don't rely on memory.

  6. Keep all promises to your prospects.

    If you said you'd call Tuesday at 2 PM, call Tuesday at 2 PM. Create a Google Calendar event so you don't miss it.

  7. Prioritize by conversion likelihood.

    Probates and foreclosures before high-equity cold outreach. Inbound leads before outbound leads. Build this instinct over time and document the patterns you see.

  8. Commit to continual learning.

    Refine your craft. Watch the Loom videos. Ask questions. The best lead managers never stop getting better.
